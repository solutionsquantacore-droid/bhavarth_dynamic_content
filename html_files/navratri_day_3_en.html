Maa Chandraghanta

Third Day of Navratri

The Divine Form of Maa Chandraghanta

On the third day of Navratri, the third form of Goddess Durga, 'Maa Chandraghanta', is worshipped. A half-moon shaped like a bell rests upon the Goddess's forehead, earning her the name Chandraghanta. She rides a majestic lion and possesses ten hands, each adorned with divine weapons. Worshipping her blesses devotees with profound courage and fearlessness.

Rituals of Worship

  • Bathing & Reverence: Bathe early in the morning, meditate upon Maa Chandraghanta, and offer your respects to the Kalash deity.
  • Attire: Maa Chandraghanta is deeply fond of red or brown clothing. Use the color red in your worship.
  • Offerings: Offer kumkum, akshat (holy rice), sindoor, and fragrant jasmine or red roses to the Divine Mother.
  • Aarti: Perform the Aarti with incense, a radiant lamp, and camphor, and recite the sacred Durga Chalisa.

Mythological Tale

According to mythology, when the terror of demons escalated and Mahishasura seized control of the celestial realms, the deeply concerned gods approached the Divine Trinity (Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva).

Hearing the plight of the gods, the Trinity was engulfed in profound wrath. From their fury emerged a divine cosmic energy, which manifested as Maa Chandraghanta. Lord Shiva granted her his Trishul (trident), Lord Vishnu his Chakra (discus), Lord Indra his Ghanta (bell), and the Sun God bestowed her with blinding radiance.

Armed with these divine weapons, Maa Chandraghanta engaged in a fierce battle with Mahishasura and his demonic army. The terrifying sound of her bell alone made the demons tremble in mortal fear. Ultimately, the Goddess vanquished Mahishasura, freeing the heavens from his tyranny.

Divine Offerings (Bhog)

Maa Chandraghanta is offered milk or milk-based sweets (such as Kheer and Peda). It is believed that offering this bhog destroys all sorrows in life and grants ultimate, supreme peace.
