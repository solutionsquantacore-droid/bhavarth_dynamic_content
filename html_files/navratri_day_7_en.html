Maa Kalaratri

Seventh Day of Navratri

Form of Maa Kalaratri

On the seventh day of Navratri, the extremely fierce form of Goddess Durga, 'Maa Kalaratri', is worshipped. Her complexion is as dark as the night, with disheveled hair and a garland flashing like lightning around her neck. Her divine vehicle is a donkey. Despite her terrifying appearance, she always bestows auspicious results, which is why she is also reverently called 'Shubhankari' (The Auspicious One).

Puja Vidhi (Worship Method)

  • Bathing & Meditation: Take a holy bath in the morning and meditate upon Maa Kalaratri. Many devotees also perform special night-time worship on this day.
  • Attire: It is considered highly auspicious to wear red or dark-colored clothes while worshipping Maa Kalaratri.
  • Offerings: Offer Kumkum, Roli, Akshat, and specifically Night-blooming Jasmine (Raatrani) or red roses to the Mother Goddess.
  • Aarti: Light incense and a lamp to perform the Aarti of the Goddess, followed by reciting the Durga Chalisa.

Mythological Tale

According to the legends, in order to slay terrifying demons like Shumbha-Nishumbha and Raktabija, Goddess Parvati transformed herself into an extremely fierce and wrathful form, which came to be known as Kalaratri.

Raktabija was a demon who possessed a boon that if even a single drop of his blood fell on the ground, a new demon resembling him would instantly spawn. This made it nearly impossible to defeat him in battle.

Then, Maa Kalaratri fiercely attacked Raktabija, and before his blood could touch the earth, she collected it in her skull-bowl (Khappar) and drank it. In this way, Raktabija met his ultimate end, liberating the celestial realms from the terror of the demons.

Divine Offering (Bhog)

Maa Kalaratri is extremely fond of offerings made of Jaggery (Gud). It is firmly believed that offering jaggery to the Goddess on this day grants liberation from all sorts of grief and fears, while providing divine protection from sudden crises.
