Maa Brahmacharini

Second Day of Navratri

Divine Form of Maa Brahmacharini

On the second day of Navratri, the 'Maa Brahmacharini' form of Goddess Durga is worshipped. 'Brahma' means penance (tapasya) and 'Charini' means one who practices it. She holds a rosary (japa mala) in Her right hand and a water pot (kamandalu) in Her left hand. This Goddess enhances penance, sacrifice, detachment, virtue, and self-control.

Puja Rituals

  • Bath & Vow (Sankalpa): Wake up early, take a bath, and take a vow for the fast or worship.
  • Kalash Puja: First, worship the Kalash deity (Lord Ganesha) and the Navagrahas.
  • Worship Materials: Bathe the Goddess with Panchamrit. Offer kumkum, akshat (unbroken rice), and yellow and red flowers (like lotus or hibiscus).
  • Aarti: Light incense and a lamp, recite the Durga Chalisa, and conclude with the Aarti of Maa Brahmacharini.

Mythological Story

According to legend, in Her previous birth, Maa Brahmacharini was born as a daughter in the house of the Himalayas. Influenced by the teachings of Sage Narada, She performed severe penance to obtain Lord Shiva as Her husband.

For thousands of years, She subsisted only on fruits and flowers, and later continued Her worship of Lord Shiva by consuming only dried leaves. Eventually, She gave up eating even leaves, earning Her the name 'Aparna'. Due to this arduous penance, the Goddess came to be known as 'Brahmacharini'. Pleased by Her devotion, Lord Brahma granted Her a boon that Lord Shiva would indeed become Her husband.

Divine Offerings (Bhog)

Maa Brahmacharini is exceedingly fond of offerings of sugar and Panchamrit. It is believed that offering sugar on this day grants a long life to family members and dispels the fear of untimely death.
