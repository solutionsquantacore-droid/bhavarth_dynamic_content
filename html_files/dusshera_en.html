Dussehra

The grand festival of the victory of good over evil and truth over untruth

Significance of Vijayadashami

Dussehra or Vijayadashami is a major festival of Hindus. It is celebrated on the Dashami tithi of the Shukla Paksha of Ashwin month. According to mythological beliefs, on this very day Lord Rama defeated Ravana and Maa Durga vanquished the demon Mahishasura.

Key Beliefs:

  • This festival symbolizes the end of ego, anger, and ignorance in the form of Ravana.
  • Worshipping the Shami tree on this day brings victory, happiness, and prosperity.
  • Sighting the Neelkanth bird on Dussehra is considered highly auspicious and lucky.

Mythological Tales of Dussehra

Lord Rama's Victory over Ravana:
The festival of Dussehra (Vijayadashami) is primarily celebrated to commemorate Lord Rama's victory over Ravana. Ravana had deceitfully abducted Sita, following which Lord Rama, along with the Vanara Sena (monkey army), waged a war on Lanka. After a fierce battle that lasted for nine days, on the tenth day (Ashwin Shukla Dashami), Lord Rama slew the arrogant and tyrannical Ravana, re-establishing righteousness (Dharma). Hence, it is celebrated as the victory of good over evil.

Maa Durga's Victory over Mahishasura:
According to another belief, a powerful demon named Mahishasura defeated the gods and took control of heaven. To put an end to his terror, Maa Durga (Mahishasuramardini) emerged from the combined cosmic energies of all the gods. A fierce battle raged between Maa Durga and Mahishasura for nine days. On the tenth day, the Goddess vanquished Mahishasura, liberating the three worlds from his wrath.

Rituals & Traditions

  • Shastra & Aparajita Puja: Special worship of weapons, vehicles, and Goddess Aparajita is performed on this day.
  • Shami Pujan: Light a lamp under the Shami tree in the evening and offer your prayers.
  • Ravan Dahan: Celebrate the end of evil by burning the effigies of Ravana, Kumbhakarna, and Meghnad.
  • Blessings of Elders: Touch the feet of the elders in your family and seek their blessings.
  • Eating Paan: There is a special tradition of eating and offering paan (betel leaves) on Dussehra, symbolizing love and respect.
