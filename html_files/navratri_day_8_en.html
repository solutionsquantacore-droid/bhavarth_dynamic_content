Maa Mahagauri

Eighth Day of Navratri (Mahashtami)

Divine Form of Maa Mahagauri

On the eighth day of Navratri (Mahashtami), 'Maa Mahagauri' is worshipped. Her complexion is radiantly fair (Gaur). Adorned in pristine white garments and luminous ornaments, she is also known as Shwetambardhara. The Goddess has four arms and her divine mount is the sacred bull (Vrishabha). Immersing oneself in her devotion absolves all sins and brings profound purity to life.

Stuti and Mantra

Om Devi Mahagauryai Namah॥
Shwete Vrishesamarudha Shwetambaradhara Shuchihi。
Mahagauri Shubham Dadyanmahadeva Pramodada॥

Worship Method and Kanya Pujan

  • Bathing and Attire: It is highly auspicious to worship the Goddess wearing pink or white garments on Mahashtami.
  • Puja Offerings: Offer white flowers (like Mogra), Roli, Akshat, and Kumkum to Maa.
  • Kanya Pujan: On Ashtami, nine little girls are worshipped as incarnations of the Goddess, fed delicious meals (Kanjak), and offered gifts to seek their divine blessings.
  • Aarti: Light incense and a sacred lamp (Diya) to perform Maa's Aarti.

Mythological Tale

According to the sacred texts, Maa Parvati performed extremely severe penance to attain Lord Shiva as her divine consort. Due to thousands of years of this intense austerity, her radiant form turned dark.

Deeply pleased with her supreme devotion, Lord Shiva accepted her and cleansed her with the holy waters of the Ganges. The divine touch of the Ganga made her complexion exceptionally luminous and brilliantly fair (Gaur), much like a streak of lightning. Since then, this breathtakingly beautiful and immaculate form of the Goddess has been revered as 'Mahagauri'.

Sacred Offering (Bhog)

Maa Mahagauri is immensely fond of Coconut offerings. It is believed that presenting a coconut to the Goddess on Ashtami eradicates all childbirth-related issues and fulfills heartfelt desires.
